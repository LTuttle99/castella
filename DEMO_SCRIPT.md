# Three-minute narrated video script

**0:00–0:25** — “Castella helps stadium teams think from ticket assumptions to an arrival range. The page separates published research findings from scenario estimates.”

**0:25–0:55** — Open Analytics. “The supplied research summary covers 5,104 games from 2000 to 2019. Random Forest had a reported cross-validated MAE of 3,045 fans. That is an average error, not a guarantee for a single game.”

**0:55–1:40** — Open Ticket simulator. Enter 35,000 tickets and 90% utilization. “This scenario estimates 31,500 arrivals and 3,500 unused tickets. The 90% rate is an assumption, not an NFL average.” Adjust low and high rates and show range.

**1:40–2:15** — Open Operations. “These estimates depend on the ratios entered here. They help compare scenarios, but they are not certified staffing or safety requirements.”

**2:15–2:45** — Ask Assistant which model performed best and whether the original model can forecast next week's game. “Answers cite the supplied evidence and acknowledge that the trained model was not provided.”

**2:45–3:00** — “Castella makes the assumptions visible. The next step for a venue pilot is validated ticket and entry-scan history.”

## Five-minute live demonstration outline

1. Overview and central decision, 30 seconds.
2. Research comparison, MAE definition, and model limitation, 60 seconds.
3. Calculate 35,000 tickets at 90%, then adjust scenario assumptions, 90 seconds.
4. Save two scenarios and compare their assumptions, 45 seconds.
5. Show operational estimates and explain their editable ratios, 60 seconds.
6. Ask the assistant a research question and close with missing data needed for deployment, 55 seconds.
